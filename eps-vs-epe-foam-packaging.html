EPS vs EPE foam: which is better for packaging?

Both are foams, both protect products in transit, and both are made at our factory. They behave very differently, and most packaging problems we see come from using one where the other belongs.

What each material is

EPS (expanded polystyrene, sold in India as thermocol) is made by expanding polystyrene beads with steam and fusing them in a mould. The result is rigid, light, and about 98% air. It holds its shape, insulates well, and is cheap per litre of volume.

EPE (expanded polyethylene) is a closed-cell foam made in sheets. It is soft and flexible, does not crumble, and springs back after it is compressed. It costs more than EPS for the same volume.

When EPS is the right choice

  • The product is heavy and needs a rigid cradle: motors, pumps, compressors, castings.
  • You need the pack to carry stacking load inside the carton.
  • Temperature matters: ice boxes, vaccine carriers, insulation sheets.
  • Volumes are high and cost per piece matters more than reuse.
  • The product's surface is not easily marked.

When EPE is the right choice

  • The surface is polished, painted, lacquered or printed and must not be scuffed.
  • The pack is opened and closed many times or returned for reuse: line-side trays, demo kits, instruments.
  • The product is light but fragile and needs a softer landing: glass, optics, electronics with displays.
  • You need a premium unboxing finish (EPE can be laminated with cloth or film).

Using both in one pack

A common and economical design is an EPS outer for structure, insulation and stacking strength, with EPE pads or an EPE lining only where the foam touches a sensitive surface. You pay for EPE only where it earns its keep.

Quick comparison
RigidityEPS rigid; EPE flexible
Surface contactEPS can abrade soft finishes; EPE is non-abrasive
Recovery after impactEPS can crush permanently; EPE recovers
InsulationEPS better
Cost per volumeEPS lower
ReuseEPE better
